2. The problem: AI works on what you hand it; work happens where you don't

Go2 starts from the actual work instead of from the artifacts people remember to log.

Today's AI tools only act on what someone explicitly hands them — a ticket, a CRM record, a chat log. The actual work — what a person does across their apps, tabs, and keystrokes all day — is invisible to those tools.

So the highest-leverage, most repetitive work never gets automated, because nobody ever wrote it down.

3. What it is, in one sentence we can defend

You put our software on someone's computer, we read what the work actually is, and we send back exactly how to fix it.

Everything beyond that is value-add we charge more for.

Mechanically:

  • Each team member runs the Cowork.ai desktop app, a local background daemon that captures app and website usage, window titles, URLs, and keystroke activity (with periodic screenshots interpreted locally via OCR).
  • Every data stream is independently toggleable, and sensitive data (credit cards, SSNs, passwords) is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk.
  • During the pilot, data lives in a local database on the worker's machine; nothing is uploaded or transmitted.
  • The data is parsed into structured form and fed into an insight engine that produces a report telling operators where time is recoverable and what to automate.

Compliance Note: The cloud version of the software (Go2 Desktop) is SOC 2 compliant. The local-only pilot daemon runs the same codebase but is not separately certified; on the pilot, your data simply never leaves the machine.

5. What the proof point does NOT prove

A skeptic's first instinct is to find the gap, so here it is from us (said before you ask):

  • The economics number ($15 / ~1 hr / no human labor) is our cost to produce a report, stated by us. It is not a margin claim across a customer base.
  • 80–85% automatable is what we observed in the data, not what is contracted, running, and saving money in production today.
  • The working tools are internal working prototypes against sample schema — they require API, auth, and data integration to run in a customer's live stack. We call them prototypes, not production tools.

What the documents do support about the foundation:

8. Who this is for, and why the existing base de-risks it

The customer profile is narrow on purpose, and the existing staffing relationships are the reason the software claims are credible.

  • ICP: Back-office knowledge workers at computers — estimating, dispatch, AR, customer service, admin, support, bookkeeping. Tech-tolerant operators running teams of roughly 5–25 people. Field labor and Fortune 500 enterprise are explicitly out of scope.
  • The strategic thesis: Sell the AI layer into existing staffing clients. Because automation can reduce headcount per client, pricing has to offset lost subscription revenue — and the staffing relationships act as distribution plus a data flywheel.
  • Go2 has an existing base of staffing clients with the software already installed — that is the wedge for distribution.

Staffing is optional to the software thesis. We are not selling it as a dependency. But the installed base is real evidence of reach into the exact ICP.

9. The go-to-market: a wedge we chose on purpose

We will tell you the failures too, because they are why the current motion is the right one.

  • What went wrong: By end of 2023 we saw AI starting to cannibalize our own staffing business. We chased a full enterprise motion and called that focus-split a mistake — not because we aren't enterprise-ready, but because it pulled us off the wedge. SMB is the wedge.
  • The structural conflict is resolved: The old staffing model had a real conflict of interest — selling staffing meant no incentive to automate away your own revenue. As a standalone product motion, that incentive conflict is gone: this is the product now. (The human side of it is still live — a lot of this work is removable, and that is not nothing when you care about the people doing it — but the business is no longer structurally fighting itself).
  • Why now: The pipeline became technically possible in December 2025 (model context-window, reasoning, and tool-use changes), and we spent roughly three months building the orchestration layer on top of it.
  • The wedge motion: Sell managed SMB pilots now — a free 2-week, one-person pilot → a paid team audit → an implementation retainer — leading with intelligence first, automation second. Not a self-serve platform launch yet.

10. The edge: the daemon and the data, not a better model

We are not claiming a better AI than the labs. The defensible piece is the proprietary telemetry daemon and the granular workflow data it captures — the stream of what work actually is, which competitors starting from tickets and CRM records do not have.

The Honest Limits:

We will be straight with you: the technical pipeline is replicable by a well-funded competitor in roughly 6–12 months, so this is not a permanent tech moat. The defensibility we are actually underwriting is speed to market, data network effects, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first/local-first brand, and our existing installed-client distribution.

  • The product is deliberately harness- and AI-provider-agnostic, built on now-standard open building blocks (skills/markdown, MCPs, AGENTS.md).
  • The framework is open; the telemetry daemon, the processing pipeline, and the insights engine stay proprietary.
  • It sits as a sidecar alongside Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, or Gemini — it does not need to own the chat window.

12. Pricing: our current thinking

Proposed, not validated

Proposed Stack

  • Tier 2: Full team audit at $500/person, $2,500 minimum (5-person), with data-period multipliers (15d 0.8x / 30d 1.0x / 60d 1.2x).
