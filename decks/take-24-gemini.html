Go2 — One Engagement,
Start to Finish

Skip the narrative and watch one real engagement run end to end. What we saw, what we built, what it cost, and what it was worth. If the before/after holds up under scrutiny, the rest of the model is just repetition.
01 / The Before

A 6-person team with a blind spot.

Battlbox is a real, delivered Go2 engagement. CEO John Roman, 6-person team.

Before we touched it, the team ran the way most back-office teams run: people moving between tools all day, with no instrumented record of where the hours actually went or which of them were recoverable.

The Honest Gap
AI tools only work on what people explicitly hand them. Go2 works from what people actually do. Until you measure the actual work, "where should we automate?" is a guess.
02 / The Installation

The same daemon, watching the real work locally.

We installed our telemetry daemon on the team's machines. It captures keystrokes, app usage, URLs, idle time, and workflow patterns locally, plus periodic screenshots interpreted on-device via OCR (~every 20s).

Sensitive data—card numbers, SSNs, passwords—is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk. Every stream is independently toggleable.

Existing Architecture
This is not a new product. It closes the loop on a system we already had. The same daemon that built a work profile to match applicants now feeds the automation insight engine. One capture layer, two purposes.
03 / The Measurement

5,343 hours observed.
Most of it automatable.

Over roughly six months we analyzed 5,343 hours of observed work across the 6 people.

The finding: 190–250 hours per month of automatable work—about 80–85% of the observed work. That is the "before" turned into a number, derived from actual work rather than estimated from a survey.

Auditable Math
The report doesn't hand you a single bravado number. Automation opportunities are ranked by impact with confidence scores, and the recoverable-hours estimate ships with its assumptions stated.
04 / The Build

Working tools, not a PDF of suggestions.

The deliverable was not a slide that says "you could automate this." Our golden rule is the opposite: don't describe what could be done—build it and deliver it.

For Battlbox we shipped a pricing calculator, a comment automation engine, an ops dashboard, and implementation playbooks. Implementation estimate: under 3 days.

Honest Scope
Because you should be skeptical: these are internal working prototypes built against sample schema. Standing them up in production requires API, auth, and data integration.
05 / The Proof Point

The before/after delta.

The single most valuable data point in any engagement is the after: leave the daemon on and measure actual time saved against the pre-automation baseline.

That before-and-after measurement is offered as an optional follow-up—the daemon stays on two weeks later to measure real recovered hours. It is the client's call, never automatic.

The Discipline
The report is a hypothesis; the post-automation measurement is the proof. We'd rather show you a measured delta than a projected one.
06 / Unit Economics

What that engagement cost us to produce.

The asymmetry is why this is a business. The market comparable: $25K–$50K, 4–8 weeks, and you get a PDF with no implementation.

Our cost to generate one audit is about $15 in raw compute and roughly an hour of processing, with no human labor beyond triggering the analysis.

Precision Over Bravado
The ~$15 is raw compute for one report. The realistic buyer-facing monthly AI/compute figure to plan against is $300–500/mo. Anchor on the honest operating number.
07 / The Conflict

Why we ran Battlbox at all.

I won't pretend Battlbox was pure altruism. We were losing $1,200/month on that account as a staffing client. The audit was the clean way to transition the team directly to John and exit staffing on good terms.

The old staffing model had a built-in conflict: selling staffing meant no incentive to automate away our own revenue.

Alignment
By end of 2023 we watched AI cannibalize existing staffing spend. Standalone, sold to the right buyer, that conflict is gone. The audit and automation are now aligned with the customer.
08 / Repeatability

The early signal: zero theater.

Battlbox is one data point. The early repeat signal: in one week we ran 6 demos and 6 of 6 moved forward.

This is demo conversion on the prospect's own data, not signed-invoice data. It converts because there's no theater: every demo runs on the buyer's actual workflow, not a canned example.

Don't Sell The Outcome
Most teams do not act on insights even when obvious—they spin in circles. We sell the audit, built tools, and a managed retainer to ensure the work actually gets implemented.
09 / The Motion

Intelligence first, automation second.

The wedge is the audit. Free 1-person, 2-week pilot as the hook; full team audit; then an implementation retainer to do the building and maintenance.

We are deliberately selling managed SMB pilots now—not a self-serve platform—keeping the core workflow-truth and control plane closed while we prove the market.

Unvalidated Pricing
Proposed stack: pilot free, team audit, monthly retainer, runtime. It is explicitly not yet validated. The posture is ship a price, learn, adjust.
10 / The Edge

The data feed is the moat.

The defensible part isn't the report—it's what it's built from. Competitors start from tickets, CRM, or chat. Go2 starts from actual work: a faster, more granular feed.

Seat and usage billers are structurally disincentivized to optimize away seats. Our incentive points the other way.

Defensibility
The pipeline is replicable in 6–12 months. The durable moat is speed to market, the data network effect, implementation lock-in, and a privacy-first posture—not the code.
11 / Compliance

Safe to run at scale: local-first.

The reason an enterprise can let us in: data lives in a local database on the worker's machine, protected by that machine's existing security. Nothing is uploaded or transmitted during the pilot.

Optional end-of-pilot zero-data-retention cloud processing is decided together, never automatic.

SOC 2 Nuance
The cloud Go2 Desktop is SOC 2 compliant. The pilot daemon is the same codebase, but data stays local. The local daemon is not separately certified; it relies on the machine's security.
12 / Enterprise Path

A credible direction, not closed proof.

We have been enterprise-ready. The deliberate choice is to run the SMB audit wedge now because it makes the IP more valuable. You can't harden enterprise and run the SMB motion at full speed in the same window.

The Scrutiny
Large conversations have tended to drift toward JV or acquisition. I'm presenting enterprise as a direction with real pull, not a signed outcome.

Read the rest the way you read Battlbox: one delivered engagement, instrumented, honest about cost and scope, repeatable as a managed motion.

The thing already exists; what's left is doing it more, with the discipline you'd demand.
