2. The problem
AI works on what you hand it; work happens where you don't
Today's AI tools only act on what someone explicitly hands them.
02
A ticket. A CRM record. A chat log.

The actual work — what a person does across their apps, tabs, and keystrokes all day — is invisible to those tools. So the highest-leverage, most repetitive work never gets automated, because nobody ever wrote it down.
Go2 starts from the actual work instead of from the artifacts people remember to log.
We deliberately do not quantify the size of this problem. Any market-size number would be invented.

3. What it is
One sentence we can defend
You put our software on someone's computer, we read what the work actually is, and we send back exactly how to fix it.
03
Step 1
Capture locally
Cowork.ai desktop app runs as a local background daemon capturing app and website usage, window titles, URLs, keystroke activity, and periodic screenshots interpreted locally via OCR.
Step 2
Filter at source
Every data stream is independently toggleable. Sensitive data — credit card numbers, SSNs, passwords — is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk.
Step 3
Keep pilot local
During the pilot the data lives in a local database on the worker's machine; nothing is uploaded or transmitted.
Step 4
Return fixes
The data is parsed into structured form and fed into an insight engine that produces a report telling operators where time is recoverable and what to automate.

Cloud scope, precisely stated

The cloud version of the software, Go2 Desktop, is SOC 2 compliant.

Pilot scope, precisely stated

The local-only pilot daemon runs the same codebase but is not separately certified; on the pilot, the data simply never leaves the machine.
Source: pricing-brief.md and Workflow Discovery Pilot one-pager, as cited in the source content.

9. Go-to-market
A wedge we chose on purpose, including the mistakes
Failures included, because they are why the current motion is the right one.
09

What went wrong

By end of 2023, AI was starting to cannibalize the staffing business. Chasing a full enterprise motion was called a focus-split mistake — not because the product was not enterprise-ready, but because it pulled the team off the wedge. SMB is the wedge.

Conflict resolved structurally

The old staffing model had a real conflict of interest: selling staffing meant no incentive to automate away your own revenue. As a standalone product motion, that structural incentive conflict is gone.

Why now

The pipeline became technically possible in December 2025 and Go2 spent roughly three months building the orchestration layer on top of it.

The wedge motion

Sell managed SMB pilots now — a free 2-week, one-person pilot → a paid team audit → an implementation retainer — leading with intelligence first, automation second. Not a self-serve platform launch yet.
Source: andy-xavier-email.md and vision-doc §13, as cited in the source content.
10. The edge
The daemon and the data, not a better model
The defensible piece is the proprietary telemetry daemon and the granular workflow data it captures.
10
  • Go2 is not claiming a better AI than the labs.
  • The proprietary asset is the telemetry daemon and the stream of what work actually is.
  • The product is deliberately harness- and AI-provider-agnostic, built on now-standard open building blocks.
  • It sits as a sidecar alongside Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, or Gemini — it does not need to own the chat window.

Honest limit, explicitly stated

The technical pipeline is replicable by a well-funded competitor in roughly 6–12 months, so this is not a permanent tech moat.
The defensibility being underwritten is speed to market, data network effects, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first/local-first brand, and existing installed-client distribution.
Source: vision-doc and pricing-brief.md sections on edge, replicability, and sidecar posture, as cited in the source content.
