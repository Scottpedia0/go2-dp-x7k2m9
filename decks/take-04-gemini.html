The Audit Is the Proof

Most AI pitches sell a vision. Go2 sells a finished audit. We install a local daemon, watch the actual work, and hand back a concrete recoverable-hours finding plus working tools — for about $15 of compute.

The problem: AI only works on what you hand it

Every AI tool on the market — Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot — works on what a person explicitly types into it. It sees the prompt, not the job.

It has no idea how the work actually happens: the copy-paste between two tabs, the manual routing, the re-prompting, the thing nobody documented because everybody just does it.

Go2 starts from the opposite end. We observe how work actually happens, explain where value leaks, and turn the stable recurring parts into maintained automation.

Competitors start from tickets, CRM records, or chat logs. We start from the actual work.

What it is: A local daemon that turns real work into an audit

The product is a lightweight background process installed on one worker's machine. It captures keystrokes, app usage, URLs, and workflow patterns, plus periodic screenshots (~every 20 seconds) read locally via OCR.

An AI agent analyzes that activity and produces a full operational audit: where time goes, what is manual, what is automatable, and how to do it.

It is explicitly not a chatbot, a dashboard, or a copilot. It is a sidecar.

We don't even use our own chat product — we port the data into Claude or ChatGPT, so if we're not going to use our own chat layer, why should the client?

The output is not advice. It is findings plus working prototype tools, implementation playbooks, and a recoverable-hours estimate.

The Golden Rule: Don't describe it, build it

A report that says "you could automate this" is worthless. The product bar is the working script, connector, or skill — with install instructions a non-technical operator can follow — actually delivered.

Internally we hold ourselves to one rule: "Don't describe what could be done. Build it and deliver it."

The methodology we teach the client is plain: Skills + MCPs + Iteration = Automation.

The bar for a finished deliverable is surprising, defensible, specific, operator-usable, and repeatable.

Proof: The recoverable-hours finding is concrete

The audit's headline output is a conservative recoverable-hours estimate, derived from observed work, not survey guesses.

  • Individual roles: 50% or more of their time is recoverable (especially for data routing and re-prompting).
  • Full ops teams: Typically 30-40% of total team hours.

These are findings off real captures. As a concrete floor: a single two-week capture on one person surfaced a minimum of 22 recoverable hours.

The recoverable-hours number is the wedge: it is specific, defensible, and reframes the conversation from "buy our software" to "here is the capacity sitting idle in your own team."

Why it works now, and why the data is the moat

The audit only works because of the raw telemetry — the actual keystroke-and-screen record of how work happens.

"It works because of that raw telemetry data, which is the hard part to come by — it wouldn't work without that data."

The technical pipeline itself is replicable by a funded competitor in 6-12 months — this is not a patent moat.

Defensibility comes from speed to market, the data network effect, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first brand, and existing client distribution.

We open-source the framework (skill format, agent-coordination, example skills) and keep proprietary the one thing that's actually hard: the telemetry daemon and the pipeline behind it.

Privacy is a feature, not a footnote

What we do is intrusive by nature, so the architecture is built privacy-first.

  • During the pilot, all data lives in a local database on the worker's machine — nothing is uploaded or transmitted.
  • Sensitive data (CC numbers, SSNs, passwords) is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk.
  • Every data stream is independently toggleable, and the worker can pause or stop anytime.

The cloud product, Go2 Desktop, is SOC 2 compliant, and the pilot daemon is the same codebase.

Precision: The local-only pilot daemon is not separately certified, and local data is protected by the machine's existing security. We state that accurately rather than implying the pilot itself is SOC-2-certified.

The motion: free audit → report → retainer

The go-to-market is a wedge, not a platform launch.

  • The hook: A free 2-week pilot on one person, one role. No strings. Total client time: ~45 minutes (scope call + install) plus review.
  • The report: Generated live, implemented together, handed over. The report and automations are the client's.
  • The expansion: A full team audit, then a managed implementation retainer.

Why give the first one free? The actual cost to generate a report is about $15 in compute — it's not a loss leader. We learn whether the report lands without hand-holding before we ever build self-serve.

The half we already had

This is not a brand-new company chasing an AI trend. This is not a new product — it just closes the loop in the product we already had.

Go2 started as a workforce placement and management business. Job applicants installed the software, which observed how they worked and generated a work profile for matching locally, where data could not leave the device.

That is how Go2 got good at local-first data handling.

The first half of the product — the capture pipe — already existed and has been operating. What's new is closing the loop: the same captured work now writes the automation.

The staffing relationship, stated plainly

We are not leading with staffing here — it is optional and software-first. For texture: that business is a profitable, month-to-month subscription model that ran through COVID and is still running.

The old staffing model carried a built-in conflict of interest: selling staffing gave us no incentive to automate away our own billable revenue. That structural conflict is resolved now, because the company's value is moving to the software and the data.

When the software was handed free to existing staffing customers, it cannibalized that revenue. The discipline we hold today: we don't sell the standalone software into the existing staffing base.

The strategic role of the staffing relationships is distribution and a data flywheel.

The enterprise path: credible, not closed

There is a real enterprise lane — a path, not a signed proof.

The deliberate decision is to run the SMB motion now and not split focus. We are selling managed SMB pilots, leading with intelligence first, and keeping the core control plane closed while we prove the market.

"We've been enterprise-ready — but you can't be enterprise-ready and run this SMB motion at the same time. SMB makes the IP far more valuable."

Our enterprise conversations have a gravitational tendency to turn into joint-venture or acquisition discussions rather than a clean product sale. We push back toward a JV and treat acquisition as a secondary option. That is interest, not a closed deal.
