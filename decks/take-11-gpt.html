Thesis

The wedge is speed: a pilot surfaces real, build-ready value on a clock measured in days, not a quarter of consulting. This is the disciplined version — what exists, what the timeline actually is, and where proof stops and the plan begins.

1. The problem

Generic AI only works on what you hand it

Every general AI tool — Claude, ChatGPT, the copilots — is downstream of a human deciding what to type in. It works on what people explicitly hand it.

That gates value on the operator already knowing where their time leaks, which is exactly what they don't know.

Go2 inverts it: it works from what people actually do, captured at the source, instead of from what someone remembered to describe.

Differentiator

Competitors start from tickets, CRM records, or chat logs; Go2 starts from the actual work.

Value waits on someone already knowing what to ask. Go2 begins before that point.

2. What it is

A local capture daemon that starts from the real work

Go2 installs a lightweight telemetry daemon on one worker's machine. It runs locally and captures how the work actually happens — every keystroke, app switch, URL, and task pattern, plus periodic screenshots interpreted on-device via OCR.

That raw stream is the input most tools never get.

Sensitive data — card numbers, SSNs, passwords — is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk, and every data stream is independently toggleable.

Capture mechanics
Keystrokes, app switches, URLs, and task patterns
Periodic screenshots, about every 20 seconds

8. Adoption

Nothing about adoption slows the buyer down. Go2 is a sidecar: it works alongside Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Gemini, internal tools, or a light Go2 surface, and does not need to own the chat window.

That removes the integration tax that stretches time to value into months for most enterprise software.

9. The skeptic's read

Where the speed claim stops

Disciplined version, no hype. The pipeline is replicable by a funded competitor in roughly 6–12 months — this is not a defensible tech moat by itself.

Defensibility comes from speed to market, data network effects, the proprietary telemetry daemon and the raw behavioral data it produces, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first local-first brand, and existing distribution.

The fast-to-value claim is true for what's delivered today; the fully self-serve version is the part still being built.
