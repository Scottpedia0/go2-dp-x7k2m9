Go2 — The Return Case for a Disciplined Investor

Strip the AI narrative away and look at the unit economics. This is the grounded version of what is proven today, what still has to be built, and what the money actually buys.

Thesis

Go2 already has a real, profitable, 8-year staffing business with negative churn and a proprietary data asset no funded competitor can buy.

Why now

The new software product turns that data into near-zero-marginal-cost intelligence and automation engagements.

Investor frame

This is the skeptic’s version: the floor, not the dream.

Grounded case
A disciplined read on unit economics, proof points, defensibility, and downside support.
1. The problem is structural, not hype

The gap is between what people say and what they actually do

02 / 12
AI tools work on what a person explicitly hands them. The real recurring work worth automating lives in the gap between the prompt and the screen.

Every AI tool on the market works on the artifact: the prompt, ticket, CRM record, or chat log. None of them work from what people actually do all day.

Go2 starts from the actual work instead of the artifact. It observes keystrokes, app usage, URLs, idle time, and task patterns, then reconstructs the real process from telemetry rather than from what someone remembers to type.

“AI tools only work on what people explicitly hand them; Go2 works from what people actually do.”
Input-data difference. The distinction is not model cleverness; it is what data the system can see.
Invisible work becomes visible. The leaks and recurring work are in the workflow, not just in the documented record.
Core framing
Go2 begins with workflow telemetry; most copilots begin with user-supplied artifacts.
2. What Go2 actually is

Two things — one of them already paying the bills

03 / 12
Go2 is a workflow-intelligence and labor-to-software conversion company.

In plain terms: Go2 observes how work actually happens, explains where value leaks, and turns stable recurring work into maintained automation.

The staffing business: a subscription remote-staffing company running profitably today. Staff are dedicated to the client, on the client’s systems, with Go2 providing the technology and a coach while the client manages the work. Pricing is roughly double the cost of the person, plus a bit for software.
The software product: the same telemetry originally built to match and place workers now feeds an insight engine that shows operators how to automate their back office and ships the working automations.

3. The edge is a data feed, not an algorithm

The defensible input is raw workflow telemetry

04 / 12
The models are commodity and the orchestration pipeline is replicable by a funded competitor in 6–12 months.

The defensible thing is the input. Go2’s edge is the raw workflow telemetry — captured locally, at the keystroke, URL, and app level — that others cannot buy or scrape.

The intelligence and automation sit on top of that feed. In Scott’s words, it works “because of that raw telemetry data, which is the hard part to come by. It wouldn’t work without that data.”

Think of it like a faster market feed: the same downstream analysis is worth more when it runs on a granular proprietary stream.
Honestly stated defensibility: speed to market, data network effects, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first brand, and existing client distribution.
Not the claim: a standalone patent moat.
Skeptical view
Commodity models on top; proprietary workflow feed underneath.

6. Why the margins are real

Compute-heavy delivery, not labor-heavy services

07 / 12
The cost to deliver an audit is dominated by compute, not labor. Analysis runs without a human in the loop beyond triggering it.

Customer acquisition cost is essentially marketing spend plus compute, because the audit itself is automated. That is why this can price like a license rather than like a services business: gross and net margins sit close together.

10. The enterprise path

Credible, not closed

11 / 12
Enterprise is a credible path, not closed proof.

What is true: enterprise conversations keep happening, and they tend to convert into joint-venture and acquisition discussions rather than simple product sales — a sign that the strategic value is recognized.

One large VC firm has had deep talks and relayed a theory of how to harden the product for enterprise and monetize compliance off larger accounts. The software is SOC 2 compliant on the cloud product used with clients.

What is not true: there is no signed enterprise contract, no term sheet, and no closed marquee logo in this deck.
Near-term choice: win the SMB motion first.
Reason: you cannot harden for enterprise and run the SMB wedge at full speed simultaneously, and SMB success makes the underlying IP more valuable.
Enterprise is optionality on top of an SMB business that has to stand on its own — and it does.
Optionality
Strategic interest exists, but the near-term plan remains disciplined and SMB-led.

What the money actually buys

That repricing is framed as discipline, not desperation: the number was set to clear, not to flatter.

The use of funds is deliberately unglamorous. The business runs at a low burn; the spend is a sales team, a marketing funnel, hardening the story, and roughly one engineering hire for enterprise security and data hardening.

Capital stance: Scott strongly favors keeping more than half as dry powder.
Historical lesson volunteered openly: time-to-recover CAC stretched longer than projected the last time they scaled.
“Dry powder makes the deployed half operate a hell of a lot more effectively.”

Grounded floor under that view: a profitable base, a real delivered product, near-zero marginal cost, and an enterprise path that is real without being oversold.
The decision: whether to size up into that floor.
