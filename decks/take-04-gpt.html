The Audit Is the Proof

Most AI pitches sell a vision. Go2 sells a finished audit.

We install a local daemon on one real worker, watch the actual work for two weeks, and hand back a concrete recoverable-hours finding plus working tools — for about $15 of compute.

The audit is both the product and the proof: it is what we already deliver, it is what closes deals, and it is the wedge into a paid retainer.

Thesis

This deck leads with what is on the ground today, not what we hope to build.

1. The problem

AI only works on what you hand it

Every AI tool on the market — Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot — works on what a person explicitly types into it. It sees the prompt, not the job.

It has no idea how the work actually happens: the copy-paste between two tabs, the manual routing, the re-prompting, the thing nobody documented because everybody just does it.

Differentiator

We start from the actual work.

Go2 starts from the opposite end. We observe how work actually happens, explain where value leaks, and turn the stable recurring parts into maintained automation.

Why it matters

Competitors start from tickets, CRM records, or chat logs. Go2 starts from the work itself.

2. What it is

A local daemon that turns real work into an audit

  • A lightweight background process installed on one worker's machine.
  • Captures keystrokes, app usage, URLs, idle time, and workflow patterns.
  • Includes periodic screenshots, about every 20 seconds, read locally via OCR.
  • An AI agent analyzes the activity and produces a full operational audit.

Output

Findings plus working prototype tools, implementation playbooks, and a recoverable-hours estimate.

It is explicitly not a chatbot, a dashboard, or a copilot. It is a sidecar — it works alongside whatever AI the team already uses and doesn't need to own the chat window.

Sidecar posture

We don't even use our own chat product — we port the data into Claude or ChatGPT, so if we're not going to use our own chat layer, why should the client?

3. The Golden Rule

Don't describe it, build it

Internal rule

Don't describe what could be done. Build it and deliver it.

A report that says “you could automate this” is worthless. The product bar is the working script, connector, or skill — with install instructions a non-technical operator can follow — actually delivered.

Skills + MCPs + Iteration = Automation

Method

“Skills” are reusable automation recipes; they group into “agents” that handle whole job functions.

Finished deliverable

“Holy-shit output,” repeatedly, with enough rigor that it stops feeling like a parlor trick — surprising, defensible, specific, operator-usable, and repeatable.

7. Why it works now

Why the data is the moat

The audit only works because of the raw telemetry — the actual keystroke-and-screen record of how work happens.

In Scott's own words: it works because of that raw telemetry data, which is the hard part to come by — it wouldn't work without that data.

Hard part

The data moat is the proprietary telemetry daemon: the capture layer is the hard part, and the analysis layer on top is comparatively easy.

The honest read on defensibility: the technical pipeline itself is replicable by a funded competitor in 6–12 months — this is not a patent moat.

  • Speed to market
  • Data network effect

Open / closed split

We open-source the framework — skill format, agent-coordination protocol, example skills — and keep proprietary the one thing that's actually hard: the telemetry daemon and the pipeline behind it.

8. Privacy

Privacy is a feature, not a footnote

Local-first pilot

During the pilot, all data lives in a local database on the worker's machine — nothing is uploaded or transmitted.

Filtering at the daemon

Sensitive data — credit-card numbers, SSNs, passwords — is detected and excluded before it ever touches disk. NSFW content is auto-detected and deleted locally.

User control

Every data stream is independently toggleable, and the worker can pause or stop anytime.

SOC 2 precision

The cloud product, Go2 Desktop, is SOC 2 compliant, and the pilot daemon is the same codebase — but the local-only pilot daemon is not separately certified.

Local data is protected by the machine's existing security. We state that accurately rather than implying the pilot itself is SOC-2-certified.

11. The staffing relationship

Stated plainly

Go2 started as a workforce placement and management business and has spent years building a software layer on top. We are not leading with staffing here, and we don't oversell it as a dependency — it is optional and software-first.

Texture, not headline

The staffing business is a profitable, month-to-month subscription model that ran through COVID and is still running.

Operating model

Staff are dedicated to the client and on the client's systems, with Go2 providing the technology and a coach rather than managing the people.

One thing a disciplined investor should know: the old staffing model carried a built-in conflict of interest — selling staffing gave us no incentive to automate away our own billable revenue.

That structural incentive conflict is resolved now, because the company's value is moving to the software and the data, not the billable seat.

We won't pretend there's no tension at all: Scott is direct that a large share of this work is removable today, and that automating away roles held by people the company cares about is a real conflict — but it is no longer a conflict that points the business in the wrong direction.

Discipline

When the software was handed free to existing staffing customers, it cannibalized that staffing revenue, so today we don't sell the standalone software into the existing staffing base.

Strategic role

The staffing relationships are distribution and a data flywheel: the AI layer can be sold into existing clients, and where it reduces headcount per client the pricing has to offset the lost subscription revenue.

12. The enterprise path

Credible, not closed

There is a real enterprise lane, and we'll describe it as exactly what it is — a path, not a signed proof.

3
Take the same audit, run at scale, into mid-sized and larger organizations once the SMB motion is proven and the security/data hardening is funded.

Why this order

We've been enterprise-ready — but you can't be enterprise-ready and run this SMB motion at the same time, and Scott is consciously choosing the SMB side because it makes the IP far more valuable.

Honest signal of pull

Enterprise conversations have a gravitational tendency to turn into joint-venture or acquisition discussions rather than a clean product sale. That is interest, not a closed deal.

We are not claiming a closed enterprise contract, a signed term sheet, or a named anchor customer.
