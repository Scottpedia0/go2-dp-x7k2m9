Go2

From Install to Implemented in Two Weeks

Generic AI tools only act on what a person explicitly hands them, so value waits on someone already knowing what to ask. Go2 starts from what people actually do — captured locally, mapped automatically, turned into working automations the client keeps. The wedge is speed: a pilot surfaces real, build-ready value on a clock measured in days, not a quarter of consulting.

01

Generic AI only works on what you hand it

Every general AI tool — Claude, ChatGPT, the copilots — is downstream of a human deciding what to type in. It works on what people explicitly hand it.

That gates value on the operator already knowing where their time leaks, which is exactly what they don't know. Go2 inverts it: it works from what people actually do, captured at the source, instead of from what someone remembered to describe. Competitors start from tickets, CRM records, or chat logs; Go2 starts from the actual work.

02

A local capture daemon that starts from the real work

Go2 installs a lightweight telemetry daemon on one worker's machine. It runs locally and captures how the work actually happens — every keystroke, app switch, URL, and task pattern, plus periodic screenshots (about every 20 seconds) interpreted on-device via OCR.

That raw stream is the input most tools never get. Sensitive data — card numbers, SSNs, passwords — is detected and excluded at the daemon level before it ever touches disk, and every data stream is independently toggleable.

03

~45 minutes of client time, two weeks to delivery

The pilot is deliberately small so value shows up fast. Two weeks, one person, one role, free, no strings.

Four phases: a ~30-minute scope call, a 15-minute install, two weeks of passive local collection where the data stays on the worker's machine, and a delivery session where the report is generated live and implemented together. Total client time required is about 45 minutes plus review. No integration, no data migration, no IT project.

04

Mapped in about a week, not a quarter

The grounded articulation of the timeline: install the software, it runs locally and just watches how the work happens, and in about a week the processes are mapped out. The first stretch is observation and mapping with no integration required.

Compare that to the alternative the buyer already knows — a consulting engagement that runs four to eight weeks and delivers a PDF with no implementation. Go2 compresses the diagnosis into days and replaces the PDF with working tools.

05

The output is built, not described

Speed only matters if what comes out is usable on arrival. The delivery bar is explicit: don't describe what could be done — build it and deliver it. A report that says "you could automate this" is worthless.

The product is the working script, connector, or skill. The deliverable is a tailored Opportunity Package: a time audit, automation opportunities ranked by impact, ready-to-use automations, a live implementation, and a recoverable-hours estimate with stated assumptions.

06

Proof the timeline holds: Battlbox

Battlbox is a real, delivered engagement. Go2 analyzed 5,343 hours of work over six months across a six-person team and surfaced 190-250 hours per month of automatable work (a deliberately conservative read of the observed work).

The implementation estimate to ship the fixes was under three days. Delivered artifacts were real working prototypes: a pricing calculator, a comment-automation engine, an ops dashboard, and implementation playbooks. End to end: observe, surface, build, hand over.

07

The motion runs cheap, demoing on client data

The economics are what make this repeatable. The cost to generate one audit is about $15 in compute and roughly an hour of processing, with no human labor beyond triggering the analysis.

The demos use the prospect's own captured data — zero theater — and so far that has landed: six demos were run in one week and all six moved forward. That is a demo conversion, not signed-invoice data, but it is the buyer reacting to their own work, not a pitch.

08

Slots in as a sidecar — no rip-and-replace delay

Nothing about adoption slows the buyer down. Go2 is a sidecar: it works alongside Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Gemini, internal tools, or a light Go2 surface, and does not need to own the chat window.

That removes the integration tax that stretches "time to value" into months for most enterprise software. It is why a connector can stand up fast: Go2 built a custom AMS360 connector that is real and shipped-but-near-working.

09

The skeptic's read: where the speed claim stops

Disciplined version, no hype. The pipeline is replicable by a funded competitor in roughly 6-12 months.

Defensibility comes from: speed to market, data network effects, the proprietary telemetry daemon and the raw behavioral data it produces, implementation lock-in, a privacy-first local-first brand, and existing distribution. The fast-to-value claim is true for what's delivered today; the fully self-serve version is the part still being built.

10

Enterprise: a credible path, not closed proof

The same speed wedge scales up, and that's where the larger story lives — but it is a path, not a signed result.

The near-term motion is deliberately SMB-first: Go2 is selling managed SMB pilots now and not splitting focus between full enterprise and SMB pilots in this window, keeping the core workflow-truth and control plane closed while proving the market. Enterprise is the next lane rather than a claim of closed enterprise revenue.
